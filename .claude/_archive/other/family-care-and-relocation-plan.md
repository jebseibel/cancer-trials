# Family Plan: Care, Move, and Stability

*A working plan for supporting my wife through stage 4 cancer treatment while relocating from Austin to Colorado Springs and securing income.*

---

## Guiding Priorities (in order)

1. **Her health and comfort come first.** Every other decision gets tested against this.
2. **Continuity of care.** One anchor oncology team, with second opinions layered on top.
3. **Financial stability.** Income and insurance must never lapse.
4. **Minimize disruption.** Each move costs energy she needs for treatment.

---

## Phase 0 — Immediate (This Week)

These can't wait for the house or the move.

- [ ] Get complete copies of all medical records, pathology reports, and imaging (request digital copies on disc/portal access)
- [ ] Confirm current insurance coverage and understand out-of-network rules, out-of-pocket max, and referral requirements
- [ ] Ask her current Austin oncologist for a treatment plan summary and a referral to a Colorado oncologist
- [ ] Schedule an intake appointment with an oncologist in Colorado **before** the move so there's no treatment gap
- [ ] Start a single shared binder/folder (physical + digital) for all medical, insurance, and financial documents
- [ ] Look into FMLA / caregiver leave options at current employment if applicable
- [ ] Identify 2–3 people (family/friends) who can be called on for specific help — meals, driving, house tasks

## Phase 1 — Healthcare Team in Colorado (Weeks 1–4)

**Goal: She has an anchor oncology team and is comfortable.**

- [ ] Establish care with a medical oncologist in the Colorado Springs / Denver area
  - UCHealth Anschutz (Aurora) is an NCI-designated comprehensive cancer center ~1.5 hrs away — strong candidate for the anchor team
  - Local Colorado Springs oncology for routine infusions/labs if the protocol allows shared care
- [ ] Ask about **palliative care referral** — this is symptom management and quality of life support alongside treatment, not end-of-life care. It measurably improves comfort and often outcomes.
- [ ] Request **remote second opinions** from 2–3 top centers based on her cancer type (MD Anderson, Mayo Clinic, MSK, Dana-Farber all offer these — records + imaging, no travel required)
- [ ] Ask every oncologist: *"Are there clinical trials she qualifies for, here or elsewhere?"*
- [ ] Set up her comfort at home: bedroom setup, mobility aids if needed, medication organization, symptom journal
- [ ] Line up mental health support for her (and honestly, for me) — oncology social workers can connect us
- [ ] Get a medical power of attorney and advance directives done while it's calm, not urgent

## Phase 2 — Sell the Austin House (Weeks 1–10, runs in parallel)

**Goal: House sold with minimum burden on her.**

- [ ] Interview 2–3 listing agents; ask about "as-is" vs. light-prep sale tradeoffs given our timeline
- [ ] Decide the prep level: given everything else, strongly consider minimal prep (deep clean, declutter, small repairs) over renovations
- [ ] Declutter room by room — sell/donate/toss as we pack (one pass, not two)
- [ ] Handle repairs the agent says actually affect sale price; skip the rest
- [ ] Professional photos, list the house
- [ ] Plan showings around treatment schedule (or list after we've already moved out — empty houses show fine)
- [ ] Review offers with agent; prioritize certainty of close over squeezing the last dollar

---

## Phase 3 — Move to Colorado (Weeks 4–10)

**Goal: One clean move, fully unpacked enough to live comfortably.**

- [ ] Get quotes from 3 moving companies (full-service pack + move is worth the money right now)
- [ ] Hand-carry: all medical records, medications, insurance documents, valuables
- [ ] Transfer prescriptions to a Colorado pharmacy before the move
- [ ] Set up utilities, internet, and mail forwarding
- [ ] Update address: insurance, banks, DMV, voter reg, subscriptions
- [ ] Unpack her spaces first: bedroom, bathroom, kitchen — comfort before boxes

---

## Phase 4 — Income: Remote Job Search (Weeks 2–12, runs in parallel)

**Goal: A stable remote job with strong health insurance.**

### 4a. Job application tool
- [ ] Build a lightweight job-application tracker/automation app
  - Scrape/aggregate remote listings (LinkedIn, Indeed, WeWorkRemotely, Otta, Wellfound)
  - Store tailored resume variants; generate customized cover letters
  - Track: applied date, status, follow-up date, contact
  - Keep it simple — the goal is applications sent, not a perfect app

### 4b. The search itself
- [ ] Update resume and LinkedIn; set LinkedIn to "open to remote work"
- [ ] Target 10–15 applications/week
- [ ] **Evaluate every offer's health insurance first**: national PPO network, out-of-network coverage, out-of-pocket max, specialty drug coverage. A slightly lower salary with a great PPO can be worth far more than the reverse.
- [ ] Ask about caregiver flexibility during interviews (framed as "flexibility for family obligations")
- [ ] Backup plans if the search runs long: COBRA from current employer, ACA marketplace plan (special enrollment triggers on moving/job loss)

## Phase 5 — Treatment Research (Ongoing)

**Goal: Always know the best available options for her specific diagnosis.**

### 5a. Research tool
- [ ] Build a cancer-treatment research app for her specific cancer type/subtype
  - Pull from ClinicalTrials.gov API (filter by diagnosis, phase, location, recruiting status)
  - Track NCI-designated cancer centers and their specialists for her cancer type
  - Save/annotate papers (PubMed API) and treatment guideline updates (NCCN guidelines)
  - Alert on new trials matching her profile
- [ ] Share findings with her oncologist rather than acting on them alone — they can tell us what she actually qualifies for

### 5b. Identify the top centers for her cancer type
- [ ] List NCI comprehensive cancer centers with recognized programs in her specific cancer
- [ ] Note which have active trials she may qualify for
- [ ] Get remote second opinions from the top 2–3 (see Phase 1)

## Phase 6 — Relocating for Treatment (Decision Framework, not a schedule)

**Reframed from "move every 3 months":** the medical consensus is that continuity with one oncology team is one of the biggest levers on quality of care. So instead of scheduled moves, we use a decision framework:

**We relocate (temporarily or permanently) only when:**
1. A specific trial or treatment she qualifies for is **only** available elsewhere, **and**
2. Her Colorado oncologist agrees it's the best option, **and**
3. We've confirmed insurance coverage and housing near the center

**Preferred alternatives before moving:**
- Remote second opinions (no travel)
- Traveling for a consult or a defined treatment course, then returning home (many trials allow local labs/scans between visits)
- Short-term furnished housing near a center for a treatment block (2–3 months), keeping Colorado as home base

**If a temporary relocation happens:**
- [ ] Keep the Colorado house as home base — don't pack it up unless the move is confirmed long-term
- [ ] Furnished short-term rental near the hospital (many cancer centers have partner housing / American Cancer Society Hope Lodge — free lodging for patients traveling for treatment)
- [ ] Coordinate records transfer and shared care between teams before arrival
- [ ] Confirm insurance network coverage at the new center **in writing** before starting

## Financial & Support Resources

- [ ] Meet with hospital **oncology social worker** — they know every assistance program
- [ ] Look into: American Cancer Society (rides, lodging), CancerCare (financial grants), the specific foundation for her cancer type, pharmaceutical patient-assistance programs for expensive drugs
- [ ] Track all medical expenses and mileage (tax deductible above threshold)
- [ ] Consider a consult with a financial planner experienced in medical situations

## Weekly Rhythm

- **Sunday:** Review this plan, update checkboxes, set the week's top 3 tasks
- **Daily:** Symptom/medication journal entry; one caregiving task, one logistics task, one job-search task — not everything every day
- **Protect time that's just us**, not cancer, not the move, not the job search
