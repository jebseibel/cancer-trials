**Only 59% of the index is eligibility criteria.** The other 41% — outcomes, interventions and

**`OUTCOME` alone is 23.5% of the index**, and it is endpoint methodology: *"SECONDARY: To
examine the risk of small intestinal hypoperfusion... univariable and multivariable analysis..."*
That text is almost never what a qualifying query wants, and there is nearly as much of it as
there is inclusion criteria.

⚠️ **This is the most likely direct cause of the reported "right trial, wrong criterion"
symptom**, and it is fixable without touching the criteria chunker at all — a source filter at
retrieval time would test the theory before any re-embed.

The 249 `ELIGIBILITY_UNPARSED` chunks are 5.4% of trials, consistent with the 5.3% unparsed rate
measured 2026-08-11.

## What a naive drop would cost — measured on a 23,535-chunk sample

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Match a boilerplate pattern | 5,832 (24.8%) |
| ...**and also carry qualifying content** | 90 (**1.5%** of boilerplate hits) |
| **Wrongly dropped** | **0.38% of all criteria** — roughly 300 chunks corpus-wide |

0.38% is low. **What matters is the shape of the loss, not the rate.** Examples of what a naive
rule would delete:

- **NCT04990921** — *"Subject has provided informed consent... Her2 overexpressed breast cancer
  must have had prior trastuzumab/pertuzumab (in the neoadjuvant/adjuvant...)"*. Consent
  boilerplate, HER2 status **and** a prior-treatment requirement in one chunk.
- **NCT07284836** — *"Patients with liver metastases from breast cancer pathologically
  confirmed... visceral tumor burden solely confined to liver metastases"*.
- **NCT07309770** — consent, age, **and** *"Diagnosed with the corresponding advanced tumor
  confirmed by histology and/or cytology"*.
- **NCT06075953** — *"Patients with a diagnosis of hormone positive DCIS who have undergone
  surgery with positive margins..."*

**The finding that should shape Phase 3:** these are not chunks where boilerplate happens to sit
beside qualifying text. They are chunks where **several criteria were merged into one because
the chunker failed to split them**. The collateral damage is concentrated exactly where chunking
is already weakest — so a merge fix removes most of the risk before the drop rule is ever
applied.

That reorders the plan: **fix merging first, then drop.** Dropping first would delete the
evidence that the merge bug exists.

## The source-filter test — run 2026-08-21, theory partly falsified

The baseline above suggested `OUTCOME` (23.5% of the index) was crowding out qualifying criteria,
and that a retrieval-time source filter would fix "right trial, wrong criterion" without a
re-embed. **That theory was tested directly against Qdrant** — using an existing chunk's stored
vector as the query, so no token, no backend call and no embedding library was needed.

### Test 1 — a clean, single-fact qualifying query

Query vector: NCT06312176's criterion *"Has unresectable locally advanced or metastatic
centrally-confirmed hormone receptor positive (HR+)/HER2- breast cancer."*

Top 25 sources: **22 `INCLUSION_CRITERION`, 1 `EXCLUSION_CRITERION`, 2 `BRIEF_SUMMARY`.**
**Zero outcome chunks**, despite outcomes being 23.5% of the index.

⚠️ **The outcome-noise theory is wrong for clean queries.** Outcome text does not look like
criteria text, so the embedding model already separates them. A source filter buys nothing here,
and the 23.5% figure is misleading as a measure of interference.

### Test 2 — a whole-profile query, which is the reported failure

Query vector: NCT07604571's criterion, which opens *"Dose escalation (IEV407 single agent and in
combination with fulvestrant or letrozole): HR+/HER2- aBC with disease progression on or
following... a CDK4/6 inhibitor..."* — four qualifying facts in one chunk.

**Unfiltered top 25: 10 `DETAILED_DESCRIPTION`, 7 `INCLUSION_CRITERION`, 3 `BRIEF_SUMMARY`,
2 `EXCLUSION_CRITERION`, 2 `OUTCOME`, 1 `ELIGIBILITY_UNPARSED`.**

More trial-design prose than criteria. And the matches show why — every one is methodology:
*"This is a first-in-human, open-label, phase I/Ib, multi-center study consisting of a dose
escalation..."*, repeated across twenty different trials.

**The mechanism, stated plainly:** the chunk opens with *"Dose escalation"*, so the embedding is
a blend of **trial-design vocabulary** and **qualifying facts**. Design language is
high-frequency and distinctive, so it dominates the vector and drags back methodology prose from
unrelated trials. The receptor status in the same sentence is drowned out.

**This is the reported symptom, reproduced.** It is not an outcome-chunk problem. It is a
*mixed-content chunk* problem.

### Test 3 — the same query, filtered to criteria sources only

Filtering to `INCLUSION_CRITERION | EXCLUSION_CRITERION | ELIGIBILITY_UNPARSED`:

| | Unfiltered | Criteria-only |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criteria chunks in top 25 | 9 | **25** |
| Prose chunks in top 25 | 15 | **0** |
| Top-15 stating receptor status | — | **7 / 15** |
| Distinct trials in top 25 | 20 | 19 |

**A real improvement, and an incomplete one.** The prose is gone, but *"Dose escalation"*,
*"Phase 1a"* and *"DOSE ESCALATION PHASE ONLY"* still lead the surviving criteria — because that
vocabulary is **inside the criteria text too**. The filter removes the prose chunks; it does not
stop design language from driving the match.

## What this changes about the plan

1. **A source filter is worth having and is nearly free** — it is one clause in
   `TrialRetrievalService.buildFilter()`, no re-embed, and `source` is already in chunk
   metadata. It should be a caller-controlled parameter, not a default, since prose is genuinely
   wanted for "what is this trial testing" queries.
2. **It is not sufficient.** The root cause is chunks that blend trial-design vocabulary with
   qualifying facts. That is a **chunking** problem and needs the re-embed.
3. **It sharpens Phase 2.** The enrichment plan was to prefix the official title onto every
   criterion. ⚠️ **Titles are full of design vocabulary** — *"A Phase 3 Randomized, Open-Label
   Study of..."* — so a naive title prefix would inject exactly the noise this test just
   identified, into every chunk in the corpus. If the title is prefixed, the design boilerplate
   should be stripped from it first, or only the disease-and-biomarker tail used.
4. **It reinforces the merge-first ordering** already recorded above. Both findings — the
   boilerplate collateral damage and this one — point at the same root cause: **chunks carrying
   more than one kind of content.**

### Cheapest next experiment

Test whether stripping design vocabulary from criteria text before embedding fixes test 2, by
re-embedding a small sample rather than the corpus. That isolates the variable without paying
~25 minutes for a wrong guess.

## The stripping test — run 2026-08-21, hypothesis confirmed but **not worth acting on**

The source-filter test above concluded that trial-design vocabulary inside criteria text was
driving bad matches, and proposed stripping it before embedding. That was tested two ways, both
against the vectors already in Qdrant — no model, no re-embed, no backend call.

### The natural experiment

Qualifying criteria chunks were split by whether they *also* contain design vocabulary
("dose escalation", "phase 1a", "first-in-human", "cohort", "open-label", "part 1"). For 12
seeds from each group, their nearest neighbours were profiled.

| | WITH design vocabulary | WITHOUT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neighbours that are prose/outcome | **14.0%** | **4.4%** |
| Neighbours stating qualifying info | **62.7%** | **78.9%** |

**Design vocabulary triples prose contamination and costs ~16 points of qualifying precision.**
The hypothesis is correct: mixed-content chunks retrieve worse, and the mechanism is exactly as
described.

### The sizing that changes the conclusion

Across **47,140** criteria chunks scanned:

| | Count | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contain design vocabulary anywhere | 960 | **2.0%** |
| ...as a strippable **leading prefix** | 204 | **0.4%** |

⚠️ **The defect is real and rare.** Stripping would improve ~200 chunks at the cost of a full
re-embed — ~25 minutes locally and **hours on prod's 1 vCPU**. That is a poor trade on its own,
and it should only ride along with a re-embed being paid for other reasons.

### The stronger reason not to strip

The leading prefixes are **not noise — they are scope**:

- `COHORT B:` *"Patients may have had no more than two prior recurrences"*
- `COHORT C:` *"Creatinine clearance (CrCl) should be calculated per institutional standard"*
- `COHORT 1 ONLY:` *"Pathologically confirmed invasive breast adenocarcinoma with documented
  ER/PR/HER2 status..."*
- `Part 1:` *"Individuals with a diagnosis of locally advanced or metastatic disease..."*

NCT07089641 states **different prior-recurrence limits for Cohort B and Cohort C**. Strip the
prefix and those become two contradictory criteria with nothing to tell them apart — the same
class of error as the `TREATMENT_NAIVE` co-occurrence bug, where meaning was lost by reading text
outside the unit that scoped it.

**Conclusion: do not strip.** The correct treatment is to keep the prefix as *text* but record
the arm/cohort scope as *metadata*, so it can inform filtering without dominating the embedding.
That is a chunker change, and it belongs with the Phase 2 re-embed rather than as its own run.

## Revised recommendation, after three tests

| Change | Effect | Cost | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source filter in `buildFilter()` | Prose 15/25 → 0/25 on whole-profile queries | One clause, **no re-embed** | ✅ **Do it now** |
| Strip design vocabulary | Fixes ~0.4% of criteria | Full re-embed | ❌ **No** — and it destroys cohort scope |
| Cohort/arm scope as metadata | Preserves meaning, enables filtering | Chunker change + re-embed | ⏳ With Phase 2 |
| Title prefix on every criterion | ⚠️ Would inject design vocabulary corpus-wide | Full re-embed | ⚠️ **Reconsider** — see above |

**The single highest-value, lowest-cost change is the source filter**, and it is the only one of
the four that needs no re-embed. Everything else should wait and be batched into one re-index.
